Coming back to a session: the kept place, and what you haven't read

In your words: When I'm not looking at a conversation, it shouldn't scroll. When I come back to it, I should be able to read the transcript without needing to find the place I last saw.

The baseline being built does that: what you last saw stays at the same place on screen, new items go below it, and a quiet "14 new below" (or G) jumps to the end. Your question is whether that is enough, or whether Synopia should also show what is read and unread. This page answers it in frames: every frame is the same invented session, coming back at 2:53 PM to a turn that wrote three screens while you were away.

A. DividerA 1px line with "New" between the last item you saw and the first you didn't. It stays until you leave the session.
B. Sidebar dotA row whose session wrote what you haven't seen gets a filled dot and a bold title.
C. A and B together RecommendedOne concept, unseen, with the dot on the Main conversation card only. To review stays separate.
D. Gutter ruleA 1px rule beside every unseen item, shrinking as you read.

Seen: an item is seen once its first line has been on screen for 1 s in a row while the Synopia window is focused and that view is shown, and everything above the furthest seen item counts as seen too.

What follows from it. Reading down with j or the trackpad sees each screen after 1 s. G and "14 new below" go to the end, so the last item is seen and so is everything above it. Flicking through sessions with Ctrl+J sees nothing, because no session stays up for 1 s. Your own message is seen when you send it, since sending shows the end. A window behind another app, or a minimised one, sees nothing.

Leaving and coming back. You leave a view when you switch session or view, or the window loses focus; you come back when it is shown in a focused window again. The baseline's "doesn't scroll" holds while you are away.

Where it lives. In the backend, beside the scroll position R3.9 already keeps (ui-state.ts, UiSession.scroll, per view: "" for the main conversation, else the subagent's or shell's id). One more record per view: seen, the furthest seen item's id (a byte offset for a shell's log), and newAt, the item the divider sits above.

Over a reload or restart. Both are kept, so the divider comes back where it was. A reload is not a coming back: it doesn't move the divider or see anything until the 1 s rule runs again in the focused window.

The sidebar's dot is derived by the backend, as it derives the state word: the main conversation has items after seen, and Claude's turn isn't running.

1. Baseline: the place is kept

You left at 2:14 PM while Claude worked, at the end of the transcript. It worked on for 27 minutes and wrote three screens. You come back at 2:53 PM.

Is the kept place enough? For the first screen, yes: you are exactly where you stopped, and the first new line sits right where the live line was. It stops being enough once you read on. After one j page the old and the new look the same, and if you scroll back up to check something, nothing tells you where "before I left" ends. It also says nothing about the other sessions: in the sidebar, "Your turn, 25 min" looks the same whether you read that turn or not.

2. A: the unread divider

The same return, with a 1px line and "New" in the gap above the first unseen item. It takes no space, so drawing or removing it moves nothing. It is placed when you come back and stays put while you read.

It stays until you leave the session, not until you reach the bottom. At the bottom you are often still reading the last screen, and scrolling back up to reread the start of the new part is the moment the line helps most. Slack keeps its line the same way.

"since 2:14 PM" is the time you left. It says how long the gap is, so you can tell a 3-minute absence from a 3-hour one before reading. Items Claude writes while you are looking get no divider: they are seen as they arrive.

3. B: unread in the sidebar

Three ways to mark a row whose session wrote what you haven't seen. "Search: rank exact title matches" finished while you were away and you haven't read it; "Changelog for 3.1" is also your turn, but you read it.

With every status

The left column has unseen output, the right column the same sessions with everything seen. While Claude's turn runs (Working, queued, compacting, rate limited, no updates) the dot stays off: the moving mark already says output is coming, and a dot on every working row would be noise. It appears when the turn ends, if you haven't seen it.

How it clears

Recommended: B1, the dot and the bold title. The dot is a shape, so it holds on the open row too, where the title is bold already. It sits on the right, away from the status mark on the left, so the two never compete: the left says what Claude is doing, the right says whether you've read it. It adds no words to the state line, which stays "Your turn, 12 min". The count in B3 reads as part of the status and fights it for the same line.

The dot is neutral ink, not blue. Blue in the sidebar already means to review (the blue check and "1 to review"), and red means needs you.

4. C: A and B together, and the background panel

The divider in the transcript and the dot in the sidebar are one concept, unseen, read from the same seen record. The open question is the background panel: subagents and shells also write output you may not have seen.

Keep them apart. Merging would force one of two wrong rules: either unseen output needs a click to clear, or a deliverable clears by being opened, which R10.2 rules out. They differ in what you owe: a look, or a decision.

Recommended: the dot on the Main conversation card only (C2), nothing on subagents and shells. A subagent's report and a shell's end already land in the main conversation as items of their own ("Report from …", the task notification), so the main conversation's unseen part covers them. Each subagent and shell view still keeps its own seen and shows its own divider when you open it.

5. D: a gutter rule beside the unseen part

Instead of one line where the new part starts, a 1px rule runs beside every unseen item, from the first one to the end.

Not recommended. It marks what is left, not where you were, and the two differ: every screen you rest on for 1 s loses its rule, so while you read the rule only ever shows on the lines that just scrolled in, and it shrinks under your eyes. The divider answers the question you came back with, "where did I stop?", and stays still. The blue rule also sits close to the prompt band's blue edge.

7. The Dock badge

The badge counts what waits on you: needs you, and deliverables to review. Unseen output doesn't wait on you, so it doesn't count, and the Dock gets no dot of its own.

8. Dark mode, recommended

The divider takes the dark link blue (#6CB6FF) on the dark panel; the sidebar stays the darkest surface, and the dot is the same light ink there. On the open row it takes the theme ink, light here too.

Recommended: C, the divider and the sidebar dot, read from one seen record per view; the dot on the Main conversation card in the panel, nothing on subagents and shells; no new key; the Dock badge unchanged.

It extends the baseline, it doesn't replace it. The kept place stays the rule for what is on screen when you come back: the divider just draws a line at the bottom of that screen. "14 new below" stays as built and counts unseen items below the screen. The baseline already watches what is on screen to count what's below; the 1 s rule reads the same observer. The seen and newAt ids go next to the baseline's scroll anchor in the same per-view record, so reload and restart come for free. The sidebar dot is one more derived flag on the session's state.

Why add anything to the baseline. The kept place finds your spot in the session you open. It can't tell you which other session finished while you were away, and it is lost after the first page you read. The dot answers the first, the divider the second.

Open questions

  1. Is 1 s on screen the right threshold? Shorter, and Ctrl+J flicking through sessions starts marking them read; longer, and a quick glance at a short reply doesn't count.
  2. Should the divider also go when you reach the bottom, or stay until you leave the session, as drawn?
  3. Should the dot show while Claude works, once a turn you haven't seen has ended under it? This page keeps it off for the whole time a turn runs.
  4. For a session a terminal holds (you may be reading it in iTerm), should its output count as seen? Synopia can't see the terminal's screen; a guess is "seen while the terminal holds it".
  5. Do you want a key for the next session with unseen output, or are the dots enough with Ctrl+J/K?
  6. Should a session be markable as read without reading it, for example with a key in the sidebar, or is G on it enough?
